# deepseek/deepseek-v4-pro (served: deepseek/deepseek-v4-pro-20260423)

**P0 (blocking): Cash and cost-basis handling for absolute‑target changes corrupts the track record.**  
Section “Decision Outcome, point 2” overwrites `avg_entry_price` with the fill price and adjusts cash only by `−(new_shares − old_shares) × fill_price`.  When a target *changes* (e.g. 5 % → 10 % of NAV), this silently resets the entire position’s cost basis to the new price, ignoring the unrealised gain/loss that must be realised on the part of the old position that is effectively replaced.  The projection’s cash balance then becomes inconsistent with the true portfolio value, and the track‑record (ADR‑0080) will be distorted.  The decision admits “realized P&L is explicitly out of scope”, but the cost basis and cash are core to the projection; simply overwriting them without a proper close/re‑open (or partial‑fill) treatment makes the state.db untrustworthy for any target change.  **Required fix:** the absolute‑target branch must correctly realise P&L on the difference (or, at minimum, preserve the existing average cost and treat the delta as a purchase/sale at the fill price).

**P1: The `reactor_name`‑keyed backfill shim is fragile when a reactor’s semantics shift.**  
Section “Decision Outcome, point 3” and “Backfill” rely on a hard‑coded set `{deterministic‑equity, paper}` at replay time.  If any of these reactors later emits genuine incremental fills (or a renamed reactor reuses the name without the flag), the projection would mis‑fold them as absolute targets, re‑introducing the inflation.  The compat shim is permanent and not gated by any record‑level flag; a future change to the reactor’s output would not be caught by the unit gate that checks only that *flag‑setting* reactors emit absolute targets.

**P1: No reconciliation plan with the settlement journal (ADR‑0010).**  
Section “Decision Outcome, point 2” states that realised P&L belongs to the settlement journal, but the projection’s cash moves computed from the absolute‑target fold (e.g. a target increase deducts only the incremental notional) may diverge from what the journal later derives if it interprets absolute‑target fills as full‑close‑and‑reopen orders.  The ADR gives no mechanism to keep cash and journal‑computed P&L in sync, risking future double‑counting or broken assertions.

**P2: The discriminator flag’s silent‑regression hole is partially left to convention.**  
Section “Consequences – Negative” notes that a future reactor forgetting the `"absolute_target"` flag falls back to additive folding.  The acceptance‑gate invariant test (“every reactor that emits an absolute target sets the flag”) is a good static check, but it requires maintaining a separate list of reactors known to emit absolute targets.  A new absolute‑target reactor added without updating that list slips past.  Mitigated, but still a process‑level fragility.
